Plenty of Novato homeowners assume a new HVAC system is mostly about the boxes: pick a brand, pick a size, bolt it in. In practice, a complete installation is three separate jobs. The equipment has to match the house, the ducts have to carry what the equipment produces, and the finished system has to be commissioned so it performs on the meter, not just on the spec sheet. That is why Novato HVAC begins every job with a load calculation for your Novato, CA home, working from square footage, insulation, window area, orientation, and air leakage. Guess at the size and you get either an oversized unit that short-cycles and leaves the air clammy or an undersized one that runs all afternoon and never catches up.

Matching is the part of equipment selection most homeowners never hear about. The coil, the condenser, and the blower carry efficiency ratings as a combination, and when the pieces are mixed, the SEER2 and AFUE numbers you paid for simply are not there. So we size to the load and then lay out the choices honestly: single-stage versus two-stage versus variable-speed, ECM blowers versus conventional motors, and whether your home is better served by a furnace, an air handler, or a heat pump. Ductwork is the other half of the story, and most installers skip it entirely. We measure static pressure, find the leaks and undersized branches, and correct them while the system is apart, because a duct system that fights the equipment will beat it every time.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a fast one part ways. Our technicians pressure-test and evacuate the line set to a micron-verified vacuum, weigh in the charge and prove it with superheat and subcooling readings, leak-test the gas side, vent to the manufacturer's spec, and trap the condensate with float-switch protection. The thermostat gets configured for your equipment's real staging rather than left on defaults. Then comes full commissioning: temperature split, temperature rise, amp draws, and static pressure, all recorded and explained to you before we haul the old system away. Why Hire a Professional for HVAC Installation?

Installation decides how the biggest machine in your Novato home will behave for the next fifteen years, and its worst mistakes are invisible at handoff. An unverified vacuum leaves moisture to breed acid inside the refrigerant circuit. A charge set by guesswork gives away efficiency month after month. A mismatched coil and condenser can never deliver the rated SEER2. Restrictive ducts force the blower to fight excess static pressure for its entire life. Add federal refrigerant rules and the safety stakes of gas piping and venting, and this is clearly not a job for guesswork.

Professional installation is a chain of verifications. Our experienced technicians size from a load calculation, install matched combinations, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, weigh the charge and prove it with superheat and subcooling, leak-test the gas side, verify draft, and set blower speeds to hold the rated temperature rise. Then we commission with recorded numbers, which become the baseline every future service visit can be checked against. That paper trail of measurements is the difference between a system that delivers its rated efficiency for fifteen years and one that merely runs, and it is standard on every installation we do.

How to Prevent HVAC Installation Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Flush the drain line before summer to keep algae from clogging it
  • Address small noises and performance drifts early, while they are still small
  • Change filters on schedule from day one; airflow protects both the coil and the exchanger
  • Keep the outdoor unit rinsed and clear so the condenser coil never runs starved
  • Save your commissioning report and share it with whoever services the system
  • Schedule two tune-ups a year: cooling in spring, heating in fall
  • Leave supply and return vents open so the system stays balanced the way it was commissioned
