How Los Angeles Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners last 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and showing symptoms, such as climbing utility bills, rooms that never quite reach temperature, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and far less stressful than an emergency one in the middle of a cold snap.

Small problems rarely stay small for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
